About

I am Yuan (Jack) Chang, a graduate student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Purdue University. I am currently conducting my master’s thesis research under the supervision of Prof. Xiaoqi (Danny) Chen in PurNet, Purdue’s computer networking research laboratory.

My research interests broadly lie at the intersection of artificial intelligence and computer systems, with particular interests in agentic language models, reinforcement learning, prompt and post-training optimization, distributed AI systems, computer networking, compilers, and security. I am especially interested in methods that use learning-based techniques to improve the efficiency, reliability, reasoning capabilities, and autonomy of computer systems and intelligent agents.

Outside of research, I enjoy golf, basketball, and soccer. I am currently interested in full-time research and engineering opportunities as well as Ph.D. programs that would allow me to continue conducting research in AI systems and related areas.

Selected Projects

Reinforcement Learning Course Project — CartPole Swing-Up with PPO

Implemented PPO for the CartPole Swing-Up problem using eight parallel environments, randomized initial conditions, and mini-batch stochastic gradient descent. The project included custom policy and value networks implemented from scratch and focused on stable and data-efficient reinforcement-learning training.

Project repository

Computer Network Systems

Implemented and evaluated several networking-system components in Python, including software-defined networking workflows, TCP congestion-control behavior, adaptive bitrate algorithms, and BBR-related mechanisms. The project emphasized understanding how protocol and control decisions influence network performance under different operating conditions.

Compiler Engineering

Designed and implemented an ANTLR-based compiler that translates C# programs through an abstract syntax tree and intermediate representation into assembly. The project provided practical experience with lexical and syntactic analysis, compiler front-end processing, intermediate program representations, assembly generation, and supporting program-analysis concepts.

IoT-Based Parking Tracker — Senior Design Project

Led a four-person team in developing an IoT parking-monitoring system using ESP32 microcontrollers, HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors, MicroPython, BLE daisy-chain communication, and a Raspberry Pi edge server. The project also included custom PCB design and was showcased at Purdue’s Spark Challenge.
